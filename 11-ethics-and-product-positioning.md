## The strategic case against the "humanizer"

You built an auto-rewrite that removes AI-detection flags. I've deliberately speced the product *without* it — not to lecture you, but because it is the one feature that would sink everything else you want:

1. **Adoption.** Your goal is official college deployment. The moment any faculty member understands that the tool also launders flagged text, the entire report becomes untrustworthy — "the machine that flags and then un-flags" is indefensible in an IQAC meeting. UGC 2018 makes institutions responsible for integrity; no institution can own a laundering tool.
2. **It fails where it matters.** Vivas, interviews, and journal review involve humans. A student who cannot explain their own humanized paper gets caught harder. The feature sells relief and delivers risk.
3. **It's an arms race you can't win** on an 8 GB laptop against billion-dollar vendors — and every month the rewriters improve, so do the detectors.
4. **The honest version wins the market you're actually in.** Colleges overpay precisely because there's no trustworthy local option. Be that option.

## The three features that replace it (and out-sell it)

### 1. Coach mode
Report shows the flagged sentence + P3 questions; the **student** rewrites; one-click re-check of just that segment; score updates. Editing history is preserved. What this gives the student: a better score *and* the ability to defend the paper in a viva — the humanizer gives neither.

### 2. Cite-assist
Most flagged "plagiarism" is really missing attribution. Matched source → P4 suggests citation placement + what a genuine paraphrase requires. One click inserts the citation placeholder in the DOCX. This turns your tool from a police product into a teaching product — faculty adopt teaching products.

### 3. AI-use disclosure log
Every action (flag viewed, citation inserted, student rewrite, re-check) appends to a JSON log; prompt P5 turns it into a one-paragraph "AI Use Disclosure" for the document's front matter. Universities worldwide are moving toward mandated disclosure; your tool makes compliance automatic. Nobody else does this — put it on the pitch slide.

## The three sentences to say when someone asks "so it removes AI plagiarism?"

1. "It finds AI-suspected passages and shows you exactly why — evidence, not a score."
2. "Then it helps the student fix them properly: rewrite in their own words with coaching, and cite sources correctly."
3. "The result is a document the student can defend in a viva — and a disclosure log proving the process was clean."

## Sample AI-use disclosure (generated by P5, template)

> *AI Use Disclosure: This document was screened with [ToolName], a college-hosted writing-integrity system. The tool flagged passages for review, suggested citation placements, and recorded revisions; all substantive content, analysis, and final wording are the author's own. A complete edit log is available to the institution on request.*

## Boundaries worth keeping

- Never market with words like "undetectable", "bypass", "pass Turnitin".
- The report always states: indicator ≠ verdict; ESL writers are disproportionately flagged by all detectors (cite doc 08's own measured FPR).
- Consent + anonymization for anything collected into the labeled datasets (doc 08).
- The archive stores submissions the college already owns — say so in the policy page, and let students see their own archived entries.

This positioning is what makes the ₹1.1 lakh ask approvable and the SIH pitch awardable: **you built the integrity infrastructure a poor college actually needs — not a cheat code.**
